| The `beyond-rpa` program computes lattice energies of organic molecular solids using a multi-level coupled-cluster approach. The objective is to generate calibration data points for simpler approximations. | **What physical quantities can be computed with beyond-rpa?** |
| Given an accuracy target, the lattice energy of a molecular crystal is partitioned into components computed at varying levels of theory. For example, nonadditive interactions require smaller basis sets and lower-order coupled-cluster approximations than pairwise interactions. The multi-level scheme exploits these observations to optimize total computational cost. | **What is the multi-level scheme?** |
| The approach required three recent methodological advances:<br><br>1. Highly accurate local coupled-cluster methods for noncovalent interactions (e.g., LNO-CCSD(T)).<br>2. A numerically robust low-level coupled-cluster method for long-range and many-body interactions (e.g., RPA+ph).<br>3. Efficient periodic Hartree-Fock implementations. | **Why hasn't the multi-level approach been more popular?** |
| RPA is the simplest coupled-cluster approximation. Despite severe simplifications to the $T$ operator, the energy model captures many-body dispersion, a key physical interaction in noncovalent systems. Nonadditive interactions are implicitly included up to arbitrary order $n>2$. | **What is the random-phase approximation?** |
| Efficiency stems from restricting the base RPA variant to a single tensor contraction topology: the direct-ring contraction. The contraction between tensors, such as wave-function amplitudes and two-electron integrals, reduces to matrix-matrix multiplication. This maps directly to compute-bound BLAS Level 3 routines, allowing highly optimized execution on modern hardware. | **Why is the random-phase approximation efficient?** |
| Base RPA violates the Pauli exclusion principle and lacks triple excitations. RPA+ph eliminates the unphysical terms up to third order. However, these limitations are compensated in the multi-level method. | **What are the limitations of RPA?** |
| The SOSEX correction minimally impacts noncovalent interaction energies in post-Hartree-Fock models. In contrast, third-order particle-hole (ph) corrections are large and systematically improve accuracy. | **Why are corrections other than SOSEX needed?** |
| Correlation energy decays rapidly and smoothly with intermolecular distance. Conversely, mean-field energy fluctuates and converges slowly, particularly in polar systems, and dominates high-order many-body interactions. Therefore, the many-body expansion (MBE) is applied exclusively to correlated wave-function terms, which are embedded within a periodic Hartree-Fock background. | **Why is a periodic boundary condition (PBC) calculation required for embedding?** |
| Density functional theory approximations introduce qualitative errors when describing many-body noncovalent interactions. Hartree-Fock theory is free from these errors. | **Why use Hartree-Fock instead of Kohn-Sham approximations as the mean-field model?** |
