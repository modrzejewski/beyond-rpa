# Frequent Questions

| Question | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **What physical quantities can be computed with beyond-rpa?** | `beyond-rpa` computes lattice energies of organic molecular solids using a multi-level coupled-cluster approach, generating calibration data for simpler approximations. |
| **What is the multi-level scheme?** | The scheme partitions a molecular crystal's lattice energy into components computed at different levels of theory based on an accuracy target. Since nonadditive interactions require smaller basis sets and lower-order coupled-cluster approximations than pairwise interactions, this approach optimizes total computational cost. |
| **Why hasn't the multi-level approach been more popular?** | It requires three recent methodological advances: (1) highly accurate local coupled-cluster methods for noncovalent interactions (e.g., LNO-CCSD(T)), (2) numerically robust low-level coupled-cluster methods for long-range and many-body interactions (e.g., RPA+ph), and (3) efficient periodic Hartree-Fock implementations. |
| **What is the random-phase approximation?** | RPA is the simplest coupled-cluster approximation. Despite severely simplifying the $T$ operator, it captures many-body dispersion, a key physical interaction in noncovalent systems. Nonadditive interactions are implicitly included up to arbitrary order $n>2$. |
| **Why is the random-phase approximation efficient?** | RPA restricts tensor contractions to a single topology: the direct-ring contraction. Contracting wave-function amplitudes and two-electron integrals reduces to matrix multiplication, which maps directly to compute-bound BLAS Level 3 routines for optimized execution on modern hardware. |
| **What are the limitations of RPA?** | Base RPA violates the Pauli exclusion principle and lacks triple excitations. RPA+ph eliminates unphysical terms up to third order, and the multi-level method compensates for any remaining limitations. |
| **Why are corrections other than SOSEX needed?** | The SOSEX correction has minimal impact on noncovalent interaction energies in post-Hartree-Fock models, whereas third-order particle-hole (ph) corrections are large and systematically improve accuracy. |
| **Why is a periodic boundary condition (PBC) calculation required for embedding?** | Correlation energy decays rapidly and smoothly with intermolecular distance, while mean-field energy fluctuates and converges slowly (especially in polar systems) and dominates high-order many-body interactions. Thus, the many-body expansion (MBE) is applied only to correlated wave-function terms embedded within a periodic Hartree-Fock background. |
| **Why use Hartree-Fock instead of Kohn-Sham approximations as the mean-field model?** | Density functional theory approximations introduce qualitative errors when describing many-body noncovalent interactions, whereas Hartree-Fock theory avoids them. |
| **What are the future directions?** | Providing a complete description of the thermodynamics of organic molecular crystals. |
